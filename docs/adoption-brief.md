# Rights by Design — Adoption Brief

*A one-page case for adopting Rights by Design as your organization's design-stage AI rights review.*

## What it is

A repeatable, sector-aware method for engineering citizen protections into an AI system **before**
it ships — privacy, due process, equal protection, transparency and contestability, and
accountability — with domain-specific modules for regulated sectors. It runs in about five minutes
against a spec, PRD, model card, or a one-sentence description, and returns a structured decision.

## The problem it solves

Rights problems in AI are cheap to fix at design and ruinous to fix after deployment — in
litigation, recalls, regulatory action, and public trust. Most teams have no consistent,
lightweight way to catch them at the design stage. Rights by Design is that checkpoint.

## What a review gives you

- An **impact tier** calibrated to what a wrong or unaccountable decision costs a person.
- A **five-dimension scorecard** (met / partial / gap) with evidence and a build-in fix for each.
- The applicable **sector module's** controls, required evidence, and deployment blockers.
- A prioritized **"build in before ship"** list with named owners and blocker flags.
- An honest **ready / conditional / not-ready** bottom line.

## Why adopt it

- **Cheap and fast.** Minutes per review, no new tooling; copy-paste or a one-command plugin install.
- **Regulator-aligned.** Maps directly to NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, the OECD
  Principles, and the U.S. Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights (see `research/standards-legislation-crosswalk.md`).
  It operationalizes the intent of the frameworks you already answer to.
- **Auditable.** Produces the traceable design-stage artifact procurement, compliance, and auditors ask for.
- **Credible.** Grounded in Rani Yadav-Ranjan's *Constitutional Democracy in the Algorithmic Age*
  (Springer, 2026); author is a 25+ year AI governance leader, law-school adjunct, and NIST
  Generative AI working-group contributor.

## How to adopt in three steps

1. **Pilot.** Run a review on one live or planned system (start with your highest-stakes model).
2. **Standardize.** Require a Rights by Design assessment as a design-stage gate for any system
   touching liberty, livelihood, safety, or essential services.
3. **Integrate.** Attach the scorecard to design reviews, procurement, and model cards; re-run on
   material change.

## Who it's for

Product, engineering, data science, policy, legal, compliance, and responsible-AI teams — and the
vendors and agencies they buy from or oversee.

## Limits

A design and review aid, not legal advice or a certification of compliance. Findings reflect only
the information provided and should prompt qualified human review, not replace it.
