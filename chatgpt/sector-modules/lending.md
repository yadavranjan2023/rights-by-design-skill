# Lending Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to credit marketing, prequalification, underwriting, pricing, limit setting, fraud controls, account management, collections, hardship decisions, foreclosure support, and adverse action.

## Decision and harm model

Lending systems affect access to capital, housing, transportation, business formation, financial stability, and reputation. Alternative data and proxy variables may reproduce historic exclusion even when protected-class fields are absent.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty

Assess necessity and authorization for:

- transaction data;
- cash-flow data;
- device and behavioral data;
- social, browsing, location, or contact data;
- education, occupation, and platform data;
- inferred vulnerability or willingness to pay.

Limit secondary use and sensitive inference.

### Due process and dignity

Verify:

- accurate input data;
- specific reasons for adverse action;
- meaningful reconsideration;
- correction of bureau and non-bureau data;
- qualified human authority;
- hardship and exceptional-circumstance review;
- consistent treatment.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination

Require:

- fair-lending analysis;
- approval, denial, pricing, limit, and default comparisons;
- geographic and proxy testing;
- intersectional analysis where feasible;
- representative validation data;
- monitoring for model drift and policy overlays;
- remediation thresholds and escalation.

### Transparency and contestability

Provide:

- specific and accurate reasons;
- source identification;
- correction channels;
- substantive appeal, not only data correction;
- timely response;
- restoration or repricing where warranted.

### Accountability

Name model owner, credit-policy owner, fair-lending owner, compliance owner, data owner, vendor owner, and final approval authority.

Track model, data, policy rules, overrides, reasons, outcomes, and complaints.

## Sector controls

- documented permissible purpose;
- data minimization;
- explainable reason architecture;
- fair-lending validation;
- proxy analysis;
- override governance;
- adverse-action quality testing;
- complaint and appeal monitoring;
- third-party model governance;
- change control;
- periodic independent review.

## Evidence required

- model development and validation;
- data dictionary and lineage;
- fair-lending test results;
- adverse-action reason testing;
- geographic analysis;
- override analysis;
- complaint records;
- policy documentation;
- vendor audit materials;
- monitoring thresholds.

## Deployment blockers

- reasons that do not accurately reflect the decision;
- no fair-lending analysis;
- ungoverned alternative data;
- substantive appeal impossible;
- unknown proxy effects;
- unvalidated pricing or limit model;
- inability to reconstruct the decision.
