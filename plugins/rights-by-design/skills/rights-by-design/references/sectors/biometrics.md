# Biometrics Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to AI systems that identify, verify, or infer from biological or behavioral signals: face, voice, gait, fingerprint, iris, vein, and behavioral or emotion recognition, used for access, identity verification, watchlisting, or classification.

## Decision and harm model

Biometric systems can trigger detention, ejection, denial of access, or an investigative action, and enable pervasive tracking. Errors are unevenly distributed across demographic groups, and biometric identifiers are largely irrevocable: a compromised face or voiceprint cannot be reissued. Non-consensual scanning also chills lawful presence and movement.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty
Assess lawful basis and consent for capture; scope of enrollment vs incidental scanning; retention of matched and non-matched templates; secondary use and sharing; and chilling effects on presence, assembly, and movement.

### Due process and dignity
Verify that a match is treated as an unverified lead; that trained human corroboration and a chance to respond precede any consequential action; and that people are not detained, ejected, or denied on the match alone.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination
Require demographic accuracy testing (skin tone, sex, age, and relevant intersections) on representative data; environmental and pose robustness; and suspension of uses where subgroup error is material.

### Transparency and contestability
Provide notice that biometric processing occurs; an understandable basis; and a documented redress, correction, and deletion path for false matches.

### Accountability and democratic control
Name the operator, data steward, and an authority to suspend; log every match and resulting action; publish a use policy; and provide independent oversight proportionate to intrusiveness.

## Sector controls

- documented necessity and less-intrusive alternatives considered;
- lawful basis or meaningful consent for capture;
- demographic and environmental accuracy testing;
- liveness and anti-spoofing measures;
- required human corroboration before consequential action;
- retention limits and deletion, especially for non-matched templates;
- challenge, correction, and redress process;
- query logging and a published use policy;
- named approval and suspension authorities.

## Evidence required

- intended-use statement and necessity analysis;
- data inventory and lawful-basis record;
- subgroup and environmental validation results;
- confidence-threshold basis;
- human-corroboration procedure and logs;
- retention and deletion records;
- redress and correction records;
- vendor terms and audit rights.

## Presumptive deployment blockers

- acting on a match without independent human corroboration;
- material subgroup error at the deployed threshold;
- no lawful authority or meaningful consent for capture;
- retention of non-matched templates or indefinite retention without necessity;
- no redress for false matches.
