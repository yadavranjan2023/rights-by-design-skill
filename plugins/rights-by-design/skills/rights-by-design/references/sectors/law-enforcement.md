# Law Enforcement Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to facial and biometric identification, predictive or hot-spot policing, automated license-plate readers, social-media and location surveillance, gunshot detection, and investigative lead-generation tools. This module governs rights, control, and oversight; it does not provide surveillance or investigative capability.

## Decision and harm model

Law-enforcement AI can drive stops, searches, and arrests, directly affecting liberty, and can concentrate enforcement on specific communities. Mass surveillance chills lawful behavior even absent charges, biometric and predictive systems have documented error and bias, and an algorithmic "lead" can wrongly become the basis for enforcement.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty
Assess suspicionless mass collection, retention, lawful basis, and chilling effects on speech, assembly, and movement.

### Due process and dignity
Verify that algorithmic output is treated as an unverified lead requiring independent corroboration before any enforcement action.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination
Test biometric error and predictive outputs by demographic group; assess enforcement feedback loops that turn past policing into "risk."

### Transparency and contestability
Ensure a person can learn (where lawful) that they were subject to the system and challenge evidence derived from it.

### Accountability and democratic control
Log every query and justification; publish a use policy; provide independent oversight and a suspension authority proportionate to intrusiveness.

## Sector controls

- lead-only rule with mandatory independent corroboration;
- human review before acting on a match or prediction;
- demographic accuracy and feedback-loop testing;
- lawful basis, minimization, and retention limits enforced technically;
- per-query logging with justification;
- public use policy and independent oversight;
- redress and evidentiary-challenge pathways.

## Evidence required

- corroboration-before-enforcement policy and action logs;
- subgroup error and feedback-loop analysis;
- lawful-basis and retention records;
- query logs and use policy;
- oversight charter and redress records.

## Presumptive deployment blockers

- treating algorithmic output as probable cause without corroboration;
- material demographic error or unaddressed enforcement feedback loops;
- suspicionless collection without lawful basis or retention limits;
- no query logging, oversight, or redress.
