# Rights by Design Methodology

## 1. Objective

Rights by Design is an upstream governance method. It converts rights and public-interest principles into system architecture, product requirements, operational procedures, procurement controls, testing, evidence, oversight, and remedies before deployment.

The method does not ask only whether a model is accurate. It asks whether the complete sociotechnical system is legitimate, contestable, governable, safe, and accountable.

## 2. Unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is the complete decision system, including:

- stated purpose;
- model and software;
- training, validation, and operating data;
- inferred attributes;
- interfaces and notices;
- operators and decision-makers;
- vendors and subcontractors;
- institutional rules and incentives;
- affected people and communities;
- downstream actions and consequences;
- correction, appeal, and remedy;
- logs, audits, monitoring, and shutdown authority.

A technically sound model can still produce an unacceptable system when embedded in a poor process.

## 3. Operating modes

### Review mode

Assess an existing or planned system and identify gaps, evidence, deployment blockers, and required fixes.

### Design mode

Translate the five dimensions and applicable sector overlays into testable requirements.

### Comparison mode

Evaluate alternatives independently before comparing them. Do not average away a critical failure.

## 4. Impact tier

### High impact

Use when the system can materially affect:

- liberty;
- safety;
- livelihood;
- legal rights;
- civic participation;
- reputation;
- employment;
- housing;
- education;
- credit or insurance;
- healthcare;
- public benefits;
- immigration;
- policing or justice;
- communications access;
- critical infrastructure;
- national security;
- mission success.

### Moderate impact

Use when the system materially influences a consequential decision or allocates meaningful opportunity, attention, resources, or operational priority, but ordinarily does not independently impose the final outcome.

### Limited impact

Use when foreseeable errors are unlikely to materially impair rights, safety, essential access, or mission outcomes.

### Tiering rule

Tier by reasonably foreseeable consequence, not by:

- model size;
- whether the system is called "assistive";
- whether the output is labeled a recommendation;
- whether a human appears somewhere in the process;
- whether protected attributes are directly collected.

## 5. Evidence model

Every finding should distinguish:

### Confirmed fact

Directly supported by supplied documentation, test results, logs, workflow descriptions, or authoritative evidence.

### Reasonable inference

A conclusion supported by facts but not directly established. Label it as an inference.

### Unknown

A material fact that has not been established.

### Recommendation

A proposed requirement or control, not an existing safeguard.

A policy statement alone is weak evidence. Stronger evidence includes:

- implemented workflow;
- system configuration;
- access-control record;
- test result;
- audit log;
- model or data documentation;
- appeal record;
- incident record;
- named decision authority;
- approved requirement;
- independent assessment.

## 6. Status definitions

### Met

Operational evidence demonstrates that the safeguard exists, is appropriately designed, and is applied.

### Partial

A safeguard exists but has material limitations, incomplete coverage, weak evidence, or inconsistent operation.

### Gap

The safeguard is absent or clearly inadequate.

### Not enough information

The available evidence does not permit a defensible conclusion.

### Not applicable

The dimension or control genuinely does not apply. Explain why. Use sparingly.

## 7. The five dimensions

### Privacy and cognitive liberty

Review collection, inference, retention, sharing, purpose limitation, surveillance, autonomy, manipulation, sensitive traits, location, behavior, and relationships.

Key test: Is the system using only what is necessary, for a defined purpose, under meaningful controls?

### Due process and algorithmic dignity

Review notice, timing, factual accuracy, human judgment, opportunity to be heard, consistent procedures, and protection against arbitrary outcomes.

Key test: Can a person meaningfully participate before or after a consequential decision, while an effective remedy remains possible?

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination

Review representation, labels, proxies, subgroup performance, intersectional effects, accessibility, disparate treatment, disparate impact, and remediation.

Key test: Has the organization looked for unequal burdens in the real operating context and acted on the results?

### Transparency and contestability

Review disclosure, explanation, input access, correction, challenge, contrary evidence, human review, timeliness, reversal, and remedy.

Key test: Can the affected person do something useful with the information provided?

### Accountability and democratic control

Review ownership, authority, logs, documentation, procurement, vendors, audits, incident handling, oversight, change control, and suspension authority.

Key test: Who can be questioned, who can correct harm, who can stop the system, and who bears responsibility?

## 8. Human control test

Do not accept "human in the loop" without answering:

- Who is the human?
- What training and competence do they have?
- What evidence do they see?
- Is uncertainty visible?
- How much time do they have?
- Are they independent from production pressure?
- Can they depart from the recommendation?
- Can they reverse an outcome?
- Is their decision documented?
- Are overrides monitored for both underuse and misuse?

## 9. Build-in-before-ship method

Prioritize requirements in this order:

1. controls hardest to retrofit;
2. controls preventing irreversible harm;
3. controls enabling later oversight;
4. operational improvements.

For each requirement identify:

- requirement;
- responsible owner;
- completion evidence;
- test or acceptance criterion;
- deployment blocker status.

## 10. Bottom-line determinations

### Ready to proceed

Material safeguards are met, evidence is sufficient, and residual risks are accepted by the proper authority.

### Proceed only with specified conditions

The system may continue only after named conditions are met or within a bounded pilot with controls.

### Not ready for deployment

One or more material or high-impact gaps remain.

### Insufficient information

Evidence is too incomplete for a defensible determination.

## 11. Limits

The method supports governance and design. It does not replace legal advice, technical validation, safety certification, regulatory approval, civil-rights review, medical review, weapons review, airworthiness review, flight readiness, command authorization, or mission assurance.
