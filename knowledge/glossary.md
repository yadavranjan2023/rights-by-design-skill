# Rights by Design Glossary

## Accountable owner

A named role with authority and responsibility for a system outcome, control, incident, or remedy.

## Adverse action

A decision or action that denies, restricts, changes, or burdens an opportunity, service, benefit, right, account, status, or resource.

## Algorithmic dignity

Treatment of affected people as persons entitled to notice, reasons, participation, correction, and meaningful human judgment rather than as passive data objects.

## Automation bias

The tendency to defer to an automated recommendation even when contrary evidence exists.

## Bounded autonomy

Autonomous operation limited by defined actions, conditions, time, geography, confidence, mission phase, authority, supervision, and prohibited behaviors.

## Cognitive liberty

Protection of mental autonomy, beliefs, attention, intention, emotion, and freedom from unjustified inference or manipulation.

## Contestability

The practical ability to challenge an outcome, correct relevant facts, present contrary evidence, obtain qualified human review, and secure reversal or remedy.

## Data lineage

A traceable record of where data originated, how it changed, where it moved, and how it was used.

## Democratic control

Oversight and authority that make public or institutionally consequential systems answerable to lawful governance, affected communities, and designated officials.

## Deployment blocker

A deficiency that must be resolved before deployment or continued operation.

## Disparate impact

A facially neutral practice that produces materially unequal effects on protected or affected groups.

## Disparate treatment

Different treatment based on a protected or sensitive characteristic.

## Evidence of completion

Documentation or operational proof showing that a required safeguard has been implemented and tested.

## Explainability

The capacity to provide a useful account of the basis, factors, limits, and uncertainty of a system output.

## Fail-safe

A design that moves to or preserves a safer state when a failure occurs.

## Graceful degradation

A system's ability to retain limited, predictable, and safer functionality when components, data, communications, or environmental conditions degrade.

## High-impact system

A system capable of materially affecting rights, safety, livelihood, essential access, national security, critical infrastructure, or mission success.

## Human override

Authorized ability to reject, reverse, stop, modify, or supersede an automated action or recommendation.

## Independent review

Assessment by a person or function sufficiently separate from development, procurement, operation, or performance incentives to exercise objective judgment.

## Inferred attribute

A characteristic derived or predicted from other data rather than directly provided.

## Meaningful human control

Human involvement with sufficient information, competence, time, independence, and authority to make or alter the consequential decision.

## Mission assurance

Disciplined activities that increase confidence that a system will perform its intended mission safely, securely, reliably, and within approved constraints.

## Model card

Documentation describing a model's purpose, intended use, limitations, performance, data, evaluation, and risk considerations.

## Nominal review

Human involvement that exists in form but lacks real information, time, competence, or authority.

## Protected audit log

A record designed to resist unauthorized alteration and preserve traceability of relevant events.

## Proxy variable

A feature that can stand in for or strongly correlate with a protected or sensitive attribute.

## Purpose limitation

Restriction of data and system use to defined, legitimate, and authorized purposes.

## Reasonable inference

A conclusion supported by available facts but not directly established.

## Red teaming

Structured testing intended to expose misuse, failure modes, adversarial vulnerabilities, or hidden assumptions.

## Remedy

An effective correction, reversal, restoration, compensation, explanation, or other response to an improper outcome.

## Representative data

Data sufficiently reflecting relevant populations, conditions, environments, and edge cases for the intended use.

## Residual risk

Risk remaining after controls have been implemented.

## Safe mode

A predetermined state intended to reduce danger and preserve recovery options during abnormal conditions.

## Sector overlay

Additional domain-specific criteria applied after the five universal Rights-by-Design dimensions.

## Sociotechnical system

The combined model, data, software, hardware, people, procedures, institutions, vendors, incentives, and consequences involved in an AI-enabled process.

## Subgroup performance

Measured system performance for defined population groups, including intersectional groups where appropriate.

## Transparency

Disclosure that makes the existence, role, operation, limits, or outcome of a system understandable.

## Uncertainty estimate

A quantitative or qualitative indication of confidence, ambiguity, data quality, or prediction reliability.

## Verification and validation

Verification asks whether the system was built according to requirements. Validation asks whether it is suitable for the intended use and environment.
