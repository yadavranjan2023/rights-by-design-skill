# Robotics Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to robots that share physical space with people: industrial and collaborative robots, autonomous mobile robots in warehouses and public spaces, delivery robots, and service or care robots, including systems that pace or monitor nearby human work.

## Decision and harm model

Robots can cause physical injury through force, speed, motion, or perception failure, and can degrade human oversight as autonomy grows. In care and social settings, vulnerable people may over-attribute understanding to the machine. Continuous sensing in homes and workplaces raises privacy concerns, and liability for autonomous physical action is often unclear.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty
Assess continuous audio/video/location capture in intimate or workplace settings; minimization to the navigation/safety purpose; and protection against repurposing sensing into surveillance.

### Due process and dignity
Where a robot informs human pace or discipline, verify human review before adverse action; in care contexts, preserve human choice, contact, and honest capability signaling (no deceptive anthropomorphism).

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination
Test any human-pacing or interaction model for disparate impact on disability and age; provide accommodation and accessible interaction.

### Transparency and contestability
Ensure robot behavior near people is legible and predictable; that any person can invoke an emergency stop; and that people can contest pace-based or interaction-based decisions.

### Accountability and democratic control
Assign a responsible human for the robot's actions; verify logging and incident reconstruction; and define liability for injury or damage.

## Sector controls

- verified safety separation, speed, and force limits;
- hardware fail-safes and broadly accessible emergency stop;
- bounded autonomy with clear human oversight;
- honest capability signaling, especially to vulnerable users;
- on-device processing and minimization of intimate-setting sensing;
- safety-bounded human pacing with ergonomic review;
- consent and dignity safeguards in care contexts;
- assigned accountability and liability for robot actions.

## Evidence required

- robot-safety validation against recognized standards;
- fail-safe and emergency-stop coverage tests;
- autonomy-bounds specification;
- human-factors and signaling evaluation;
- pacing disparate-impact analysis where applicable;
- sensing data map and minimization record;
- incident-reconstruction and liability model.

## Presumptive deployment blockers

- unverified physical safety limits near people;
- absent or inaccessible emergency stop;
- unbounded autonomy with no accountable human;
- human pace set unsafely by robot throughput;
- deceptive anthropomorphism toward vulnerable users.
