# Education Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to admissions and financial-aid scoring, automated grading and essay scoring, remote proctoring, early-warning and dropout-risk systems, adaptive tutoring, and student-conduct analytics. Subjects are frequently minors or young adults.

## Decision and harm model

Education AI affects academic standing, opportunity, discipline, and reputation, often for young people who cannot easily contest institutional decisions. Proctoring can treat students as suspects; risk labels can become self-fulfilling and follow a student across years; and children's data is uniquely sensitive.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty
Assess capture of home environment and biometric-adjacent data, minimization to the educational purpose, consent (including parental where required), and vendor secondary-use limits.

### Due process and dignity
Verify human adjudication before any academic-integrity finding, grade penalty, hold, or discipline, and a real appeal.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination
Test proctoring flags and scoring for bias against disability, skin tone, neurodivergence, and language background; provide accommodations and non-proctored alternatives.

### Transparency and contestability
Explain flags, grades, and risk labels in accessible terms; provide notice and an appeal that can reverse.

### Accountability and democratic control
Secure vendor audit rights; govern risk-label retention to prevent permanent, self-reinforcing labels; name an accountable owner.

## Sector controls

- human adjudication of every consequential flag or label;
- bias testing across disability, appearance, neurodivergence, and language;
- accommodations and non-proctored alternatives;
- data minimization and required consent, including for minors;
- vendor secondary-use limits and audit rights;
- retention limits preventing self-reinforcing labels;
- accessible notice and appeal.

## Evidence required

- bias-test results for proctoring and scoring tools;
- adjudication and appeal workflow;
- consent and data-minimization records;
- accommodation and alternative-path documentation;
- vendor terms and retention policy.

## Presumptive deployment blockers

- automated academic-integrity penalties without human adjudication;
- untested bias in proctoring or scoring;
- children's data collected beyond purpose or without required consent;
- persistent risk labels that drive automated holds without human judgment.
