# Smart Cities and IoT Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to public-space sensing (cameras, sensors), connected mobility and traffic systems, smart utilities and metering, environmental and crowd analytics, and civic platforms that fuse municipal data across departments.

## Decision and harm model

Pervasive public-space sensing operates largely without individual consent and can enable tracking, chill lawful assembly and movement, and be repurposed from civic optimization into surveillance and enforcement. Sensor and service placement has distributional effects, and "anonymous" data is often re-identifiable through fusion.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty
Assess re-identification risk of retained traces; default to on-device processing, aggregation, and minimization; and evaluate chilling effects on assembly and movement.

### Due process and dignity
Where fused data drives enforcement or service decisions, verify notice and a recourse path for affected people.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination
Test sensor, surveillance, and service placement for spatial and demographic equity, including over-surveillance and neglect.

### Transparency and contestability
Require public notice, meaningful consultation, and published data-governance and retention policies; enable the public to contest the program.

### Accountability and democratic control
Enforce purpose limitation technically and contractually against surveillance/enforcement creep; provide independent oversight and a suspension authority; and require open, auditable analytics for decisions affecting the public.

## Sector controls

- privacy-by-design: on-device processing, aggregation, minimization;
- re-identification risk assessment;
- public notice, consultation, and published governance;
- retention limits and deletion;
- equity review of sensor and service placement;
- purpose limitation with technical and contractual barriers to enforcement repurposing;
- vendor transparency and audit rights;
- oversight body and suspension authority.

## Evidence required

- privacy-impact assessment and re-identification analysis;
- data inventory and retention schedule;
- public-consultation record and governance policy;
- placement equity analysis;
- purpose-limitation controls;
- vendor terms and audit rights.

## Presumptive deployment blockers

- retained, joinable, re-identifiable movement traces without necessity;
- civic data repurposed for surveillance or enforcement without safeguards;
- no public notice, consultation, or governance policy;
- inequitable sensing concentration without justification;
- no oversight or authority to suspend the program.
