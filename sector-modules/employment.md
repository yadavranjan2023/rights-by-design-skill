# Employment Sector Module

## Scope

Apply to recruitment, resume screening, candidate ranking, interviewing, background review, compensation, scheduling, performance evaluation, promotion, discipline, productivity monitoring, retention, and termination.

## Decision and harm model

Employment systems can affect livelihood, reputation, mobility, privacy, bargaining power, and equal opportunity. Systems labeled "recommendation tools" may be high impact when managers routinely follow their outputs.

## Rights-specific review

### Privacy and cognitive liberty

Assess:

- collection from resumes, assessments, video, audio, email, calendars, devices, location, productivity tools, and social media;
- emotion, personality, attention, health, disability, or intent inference;
- off-duty and remote-work surveillance;
- necessity and proportionality;
- notice, consent where appropriate, retention, and secondary use.

### Due process and dignity

Verify:

- notice before consequential use;
- job-related reasons;
- opportunity to correct records;
- meaningful recruiter or manager judgment;
- consistent procedures;
- review before rejection, discipline, or termination;
- protection against self-reinforcing performance labels.

### Equal protection and nondiscrimination

Require:

- job-related validation;
- subgroup and intersectional testing;
- accessibility and accommodation review;
- analysis of school, ZIP code, employment-gap, name, language, disability, and caregiving proxies;
- monitoring of selection, promotion, pay, discipline, and termination outcomes;
- defined remediation thresholds.

### Transparency and contestability

Provide:

- understandable notice of AI use;
- explanation of material factors;
- data correction;
- accommodation path;
- qualified human review;
- ability to reconsider the outcome;
- retention and response timelines.

### Accountability

Name owners across HR, Talent Acquisition, business management, Data Science, Legal, Privacy, Security, and vendor management.

Log model version, input sources, recommendation, human decision, override, reason, and outcome.

## Sector controls

- validated job criteria;
- standardized and documented human review;
- accommodation and alternative-assessment pathways;
- ban or strict control on unsupported emotion or personality inference;
- applicant and employee notice;
- adverse-impact testing;
- vendor audit rights;
- model-change approval;
- appeal and correction;
- periodic revalidation.

## Evidence required

- job analysis;
- validation study;
- subgroup results;
- accessibility assessment;
- data inventory;
- applicant notice;
- reviewer training;
- override logs;
- appeal records;
- vendor documentation;
- monitoring plan.

## Deployment blockers

- no job-related validation;
- no subgroup impact analysis;
- inaccessible assessment without accommodation;
- covert or disproportionate monitoring;
- fully automated rejection, discipline, or termination without meaningful review;
- no correction or appeal process;
- unsupported emotion, personality, or intent inference used consequentially.
