# Example: Executive Board Review of an AI Portfolio


> **Use note:** This is a representative example for training, implementation, and evaluation. It is not a legal determination, regulatory approval, safety certification, or assessment of a named organization.


## 1. Executive summary

The board reviews an enterprise portfolio of 28 AI systems, including seven high-impact systems in employment, lending, healthcare, and customer access.

## 2. Board-level questions

- Which systems materially affect rights, safety, or essential access?
- Which systems have unresolved deployment blockers?
- Who owns each high-impact system?
- Can management reconstruct consequential decisions?
- Are appeals and remedies operational?
- Are vendors subject to audit and change control?
- What risks require investment, restriction, or suspension?
- What residual risk is management asking the board to accept?

## 3. Portfolio summary

| Category | Count |
|---|---:|
| High impact | 7 |
| Moderate impact | 11 |
| Limited impact | 10 |
| Systems with unresolved blockers | 4 |
| Systems lacking complete owner assignment | 3 |
| Systems with overdue independent review | 2 |

## 4. Material findings

1. Two employment systems lack current subgroup validation.
2. One lending model has weak adverse-action reason fidelity.
3. A healthcare tool lacks tested downtime fallback.
4. Three vendors can update models without prior approval.
5. Portfolio incident reporting is inconsistent.

## 5. Board actions

- Require remediation deadlines for all blockers.
- Prohibit deployment of new high-impact systems without formal review.
- Require named executive ownership.
- Approve centralized incident and model-change governance.
- Require quarterly portfolio reporting.
- Direct independent review of the highest-risk systems.
- Confirm management authority to suspend systems immediately.

## 6. Required board reporting

- High-impact inventory
- Blocker status
- Incidents and complaints
- Subgroup outcomes
- Appeals and reversals
- Model changes
- Vendor risks
- Residual-risk acceptances
- Suspensions and exceptions

## 7. Bottom line

The board should not approve a generic “AI strategy” without system-level evidence. Oversight must connect risk to owners, controls, evidence, investment, and stop authority.
