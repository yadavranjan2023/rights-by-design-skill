# Example: Rights-by-Design AI Procurement RFP Requirements


> **Use note:** This is a representative example for training, implementation, and evaluation. It is not a legal determination, regulatory approval, safety certification, or assessment of a named organization.


## 1. Purpose

This example provides mandatory RFP requirements for acquisition of a high-impact AI system.

## 2. Vendor response requirements

The bidder shall provide:

1. Intended, permitted, and prohibited uses
2. System architecture and data flow
3. Training and validation data description
4. Subgroup and intersectional performance
5. Known limitations and failure modes
6. Human-control design
7. Explanation and contestability support
8. Logging and reconstruction
9. Privacy, security, and retention controls
10. Accessibility testing
11. Incident history
12. Model-change and release process
13. Subprocessor and supply-chain information
14. Exit, portability, and deletion plan

## 3. Mandatory functional requirements

- The system shall preserve human authority for consequential decisions.
- The system shall log inputs, model version, output, confidence, and final action.
- The system shall support correction, appeal, and reversal.
- The system shall expose uncertainty and relevant source evidence.
- The system shall support suspension, rollback, and safe fallback.
- The system shall not use customer data for unrelated model training without express authorization.

## 4. Evaluation rubric

| Area | Points |
|---|---:|
| Rights and human impact | 20 |
| Technical validation | 15 |
| Equality and accessibility | 15 |
| Transparency and contestability | 15 |
| Accountability and audit | 15 |
| Privacy and security | 10 |
| Operations and change control | 5 |
| Cost | 5 |

## 5. Disqualifying conditions

- Refusal to disclose material limitations
- No audit rights
- No subgroup evidence for high-impact use
- No incident notification
- No change-control commitments
- No support for appeal and reconstruction
- Unacceptable secondary data use
